Service families

Five useful ways to organize a request.

Each family explains common situations, the details that help and the boundaries that may affect the next step.

01

Walls, touch-ups & finish repair

Small drywall patches, dents, wall marks, surface preparation and localized paint touch-ups. Useful photos include a wide view, close-up and size reference. Widespread moisture, structural movement or extensive finish matching may require another scope.

Start with photos →
02

Doors, trim & hardware

Door alignment, handles, hinges, latches, trim gaps, baseboard details and small finish repairs. Show the full opening, hardware, contact point and visible wear. Structural framing or major exterior-door replacement may need a different provider.

Request review →
03

Assembly & small installations

Furniture assembly, shelving and straightforward mounted-item requests. Include product links, measurements, wall type and access. Specialty anchoring, concealed utilities or regulated work must be reviewed separately.

Request review →
04

Mounting & fixture review

TV bracket planning and selected fixture-replacement requests reviewed by existing conditions. Show the product, mounting surface, nearby power and access. Electrical, plumbing or permit-heavy scope may require a licensed specialist.

Share context →
05

Punch-list & home-detail projects

Several smaller repair, adjustment and finish items grouped into one organized request. A numbered list and one or two photos per item make the first review more useful.

Specialist boundaries

Not every project belongs in the same service path.

Electrical, plumbing, structural, roofing and permit-heavy work may require a licensed trade specialist or contractor. The site is designed to identify that boundary rather than blur it.
