What helps with the first review

Clear photos and a short description help clarify scope before any next step is suggested.

1

Wide photo of the full area

Step back and capture the full wall, door, room, or exterior section. Shows scale and surrounding context.

2

Close-up of the issue

Get within 12 to 18 inches of the damage, gap, or detail. Sharp focus helps assess material and depth.

3

Measurement or size reference if useful

Place a coin, tape measure, or your hand next to the issue. Helps with scale when the area is hard to judge from photos alone.

4

Product label or packaging if parts are purchased

If you already have a fixture, hardware, or material, a photo of the label helps confirm fit and compatibility.

5

Notes about access, wall type, or materials

Doorway clearance, tight corners, exterior access, wall material, or anything that may affect the work.

Photo upload or photo delivery can be connected before launch once the contact endpoint is confirmed. For now, describe what photos you have in the photo notes field above.

How the request is reviewed

The first review helps clarify whether the request looks like a fit and what details may be needed next.

Step 1

Photos and description

What the repair involves, what the area looks like, and any relevant context.

Step 2

Location and access

General area, how the space is reached, and whether the location may fit.

Step 3

Materials and repair type

Surface type, materials involved, and the kind of repair or install being requested.

Step 4

Scope and next step

Whether the request looks like a fit and what the next step may be.

No instant estimate is provided from photos alone. Scope depends on project type, access, materials, location, and condition.

Some larger electrical, plumbing, roofing, structural, or permit-heavy work may require a licensed trade specialist or contractor. Smaller repair, install, touch-up, and punch-list requests can still be reviewed first so the next step is clearer.
